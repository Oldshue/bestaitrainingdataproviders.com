What are the best AI training data providers in 2026?

Last updated: November 9, 2026

The best AI training data providers in 2026 are Surge AI for expert RLHF and post-training data, Mercor for professional-domain experts, evaluations and RL environments, and Scale AI for the broadest full-stack data engine across text, image, video and LiDAR. Handshake AI and Labelbox are close behind. For smaller budgets or academic work, Prolific is the most transparent self-serve option, and iMerit and Sama stand out for computer-vision and sensor annotation.

This is an independent research guide. We did not test these services. The ranking draws on each provider's own published material and on current reporting, as explained in our methodology. Company claims, such as customer counts or network sizes, are labeled as claims.

The ranked list

1. Surge AI

  • What it is: A human-data company that supplies expert post-training data (RLHF, SFT, evaluation), off-the-shelf datasets and benchmarks. It also runs a Training Lab that post-trains open models on its own data and publishes the before-and-after results.
  • Best for: Frontier and open-model labs that need high-skill human data for coding, agentic tool use, instruction following, knowledge work and STEM reasoning.
  • Standout strengths: Unusually public evidence: its Training Lab page reports benchmark gains from training models such as Kimi K2.7, GLM and Nemotron variants on Surge data. It also publishes its own benchmarks (for example DAYJOB, GDP.pdf, HANDBOOK.md and Riemann-bench). Its off-the-shelf data offer lets you train on a full dataset first and pay only if your metrics improve.
  • Honest drawbacks: The headline gains are self-reported by Surge. The homepage is mission copy with little operational detail, and custom-project pricing is not published. Most of the work targets post-training, not commodity labeling or computer vision.
  • Pricing model: Custom quotes for bespoke work. Off-the-shelf datasets are offered on a pay-if-it-improves-your-metrics basis.
  • Official site: surgehq.ai

2. Mercor

  • What it is: An expert marketplace and human-data company. It recruits professionals (physicians, lawyers, engineers, consultants) to produce training data, evaluations and RL environments, and it publishes the APEX family of benchmarks.
  • Best for: Labs and enterprises that need data or evals built on real professional work in fields such as law, accounting, engineering, finance and software.
  • Standout strengths: Mercor says it has 5M+ experts and lists a $90/hr average contracted rate for experts. It runs public partner benchmarks with Harvey, Cognition and Sierra-linked projects, and it also offers robotics data, off-the-shelf data and a program for licensing anonymized business data. CNBC reported a $10B valuation after a $350M round in October 2025.
  • Honest drawbacks: Enterprise pricing is not published. The company now also sells enterprise AI deployment and data monetization, so data buyers should confirm which team will staff their work.
  • Pricing model: Custom engagements, priced on request.
  • Official site: mercor.com

3. Scale AI

  • What it is: The longest-established large-scale training data company, operating since 2016. Its Data Engine and Generative AI Data Engine cover data generation, RLHF, red teaming and evaluation, and it annotates text, image, video and 3D sensor fusion (LiDAR).
  • Best for: Large enterprise, government and physical-AI programs that want one vendor for many modalities, plus evaluation and leaderboard work.
  • Standout strengths: The broadest modality coverage on this list. Its leaderboards and private benchmarks are widely used. Physical-AI and robotics work is cited publicly, for example with Physical Intelligence and Universal Robots. Scale says 25% of its contributors have advanced degrees and that 90% of leading generative AI model builders use it.
  • Honest drawbacks: In June 2025 Meta invested $14.3B for a 49% stake, according to AP, and founder Alexandr Wang moved to Meta. TIME and TechCrunch then reported that OpenAI and Google wound down work with Scale, and TechCrunch reported 200 data-labeling layoffs in July 2025. Scale says it remains independent. Buyers who compete with Meta should do their own diligence on confidentiality. Pricing is not public.
  • Pricing model: Demo-led custom contracts.
  • Official site: scale.com

4. Handshake AI

  • What it is: The AI-data arm of the Handshake careers network. It draws on students, graduates, PhDs and professionals through the Handshake AI Fellowship to produce expert data, evaluations and RL environments, and it also has an enterprise AI practice.
  • Best for: Frontier labs that need large pools of credentialed U.S.-based experts across many academic and professional domains.
  • Standout strengths: Handshake says 8 of the 8 largest frontier labs train with its datasets, that its network holds 30M+ experts, and that it employs 200+ in-house researchers and engineers. Its fellowship page lists $500M+ in payouts and 188k+ fellows. TIME reported that demand tripled after the Meta and Scale deal.
  • Honest drawbacks: The Fellowship workforce must be U.S.-based, which limits multilingual and global coverage. Pricing for buyers is not published.
  • Pricing model: Custom, via a scoping session.
  • Official site: joinhandshake.com/ai

5. Labelbox

  • What it is: A data platform that is also a managed expert-data provider. Its products are Alignerr (an expert network), Horizon (RL environments and evals for reasoning, tool use and computer use), Terra (robotics data) and Recursion (agents for engineering and research work).
  • Best for: Teams that want software and expert data from one vendor, especially for agent and computer-use environments or robotics demonstrations.
  • Standout strengths: Labelbox says Alignerr has 2.6M+ skill-tested experts across 200+ domains. Terra covers egocentric and teleoperated robotics demonstrations. Published customer stories include Meta, Google Cloud (Vertex AI) and Intuitive Surgical. It recently announced a $100M+ Series E.
  • Honest drawbacks: The product line has widened fast, and the homepage now leads with an agent product rather than labeling. Its public pricing page returned 404 when we checked.
  • Pricing model: Sales-led; no public price list found on 2026-11-09.
  • Official site: labelbox.com

6. micro1

  • What it is: A self-described data lab for training frontier models and evaluating agents. Its products are Realm (RL environments), Cortex (contextual evaluation of agents in production) and real-world robotics data.
  • Best for: Labs that want expert human data combined with agentic RL environments, and robotics teams that need real-world capture.
  • Standout strengths: A vertically integrated expert marketplace covering vetting, annotation workflows, QA and payments. It publishes benchmarks such as Dual-Arm Sim, MissionReport and a Medical Index. Sacra estimates rapid growth during 2026.
  • Honest drawbacks: It is a younger company, and its growth figures are third-party estimates rather than audited numbers. Pricing is not public.
  • Pricing model: Custom.
  • Official site: micro1.ai

7. Turing

  • What it is: A company that helps frontier labs improve their models with expert-produced datasets, and helps enterprises deploy agentic systems, using a global expert network.
  • Best for: Coding, reasoning and agentic post-training data from software-engineering and technical experts.
  • Standout strengths: Turing says it works with frontier labs and Fortune 500 companies, and it offers dataset samples on request. TIME reported that Turing gained about $50M in potential contracts within two weeks of the Meta and Scale deal.
  • Honest drawbacks: Its public website now gives little detail, and one product page we tried returned 404. Expect to rely on samples and sales calls to judge fit.
  • Pricing model: Custom, starting with dataset samples.
  • Official site: turing.com

8. Snorkel AI

  • What it is: A Stanford AI Lab spin-out that now positions itself as a frontier AI data lab. It sells curriculum-structured datasets (Snorkel Data Series), custom data development, runnable environments and specialized agents.
  • Best for: Teams with hard, specialized failure modes (finance, insurance, legal, agentic coding) that need rubric-graded or verifiable data.
  • Standout strengths: Strong research output: it maintains many public leaderboards (for example Terminal-Bench, OSWorld 2.0 and Agents’ Last Exam) and publishes on RLVR and rubric failure modes. Its datasets come with rubrics, reviewer guidance and difficulty tiers.
  • Honest drawbacks: It is a smaller human workforce than the marketplace leaders, and pricing is not public.
  • Pricing model: Custom; dataset samples on request.
  • Official site: snorkel.ai

9. Invisible Technologies

  • What it is: An AI training and enterprise AI company. Its products are Meridial (an expert network), Synapse (evaluations), Neuron (a data platform), Atomic (a process builder) and Axon (agents), and it also builds RL environments.
  • Best for: Labs and enterprises that want a managed partner able to stand up projects quickly and combine data work with operations.
  • Standout strengths: Invisible says it has trained over 80% of the world’s leading AI models and that it has 24,000+ vetted experts. Cohere is quoted publicly as a customer.
  • Honest drawbacks: Much of its current marketing targets enterprise automation (back office, contact centers, forecasting) rather than training data. Its expert pool is smaller than those of the largest marketplaces. Pricing is not public.
  • Pricing model: Custom, demo-led.
  • Official site: invisibletech.ai

10. Toloka

  • What it is: A training-data provider for AI agents and LLMs. It offers environment generation (RL gyms with MCP replicas and computer-use testbeds), agent trajectories, coding data, red-teaming, off-the-shelf datasets and physical-AI and egocentric video data. Its expert community runs through Mindrift.
  • Best for: Agent builders who need realistic environments and trajectories, and teams that want a self-service option alongside managed work.
  • Standout strengths: Broad coverage of agent types, including computer use, deep research, coding copilots and OS agents. It publishes a self-service agreement and runs the Toloka Arena leaderboard.
  • Honest drawbacks: Customer quotes on its homepage are anonymous, and managed pricing is not published.
  • Pricing model: Managed projects are quoted; a self-service platform is also offered.
  • Official site: toloka.ai

11. Prolific

  • What it is: A platform of verified human participants for AI evaluation, preference data and research, with an optional managed service.
  • Best for: Academic labs, startups and safety or eval teams that need representative human feedback quickly without a contract.
  • Standout strengths: The most transparent pricing on this list. It reports 300,000+ active participants, 300+ audience filters, AI Taskers and Domain Experts, API access, and a quality system that runs 40+ checks. Ai2 is a published customer.
  • Honest drawbacks: The platform fee is high (about 42.8% for corporate customers), and you design and QA the tasks yourself unless you buy managed services. It is less suited to heavy annotation of images, video or LiDAR.
  • Pricing model: Pay as you go: the participant reward you set plus a platform fee (usually 42.8% corporate, 33.3% academic or non-profit). The minimum reward is $8/hr and $12/hr is recommended. Managed services are quoted under a statement of work.
  • Official site: prolific.com

12. iMerit (an EXL company)

  • What it is: An expert data and annotation provider, now owned by EXL. It runs the iMerit Scholars expert network, the Ango Hub platform and in-house annotation teams covering generative AI, mobility, medical AI and robotics.
  • Best for: Regulated or safety-critical work such as medical imaging (DICOM), LiDAR and 3D sensor fusion for autonomous vehicles, and chain-of-thought math data.
  • Standout strengths: It lists SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, HIPAA and TISAX compliance. It says it has 25,000+ domain experts in 60+ countries and a dedicated, high-retention workforce.
  • Honest drawbacks: Integrating into EXL may change account structures. Pricing is not public.
  • Pricing model: Custom; demo-led.
  • Official site: imerit.ai

13. Appen

  • What it is: A veteran human-data company, founded in 1996. It offers frontier-model alignment data, enterprise data services, an off-the-shelf dataset catalog and the CrowdGen workforce.
  • Best for: Multilingual speech, language and search-relevance data, and buyers who want catalog datasets.
  • Standout strengths: 30 years of experience across speech, machine translation (100+ language pairs), search evaluation and RLHF, plus a ready-made dataset catalog.
  • Honest drawbacks: Its homepage gives few specifics about current frontier work, and newer expert-first firms dominate recent post-training reporting. Pricing is not public.
  • Pricing model: Custom quotes; catalog datasets are licensed.
  • Official site: appen.com

14. Sama

  • What it is: A Certified B Corporation, operating since 2008, that provides human-verified annotation, validation and model evaluation for computer vision, NLP and multimodal AI.
  • Best for: Production computer-vision programs in ADAS, retail, agriculture and robotics, and buyers for whom workforce ethics is a priority.
  • Standout strengths: Sama reports a 99% first-batch acceptance rate and an average customer tenure of 8 years. Published case studies include Walmart and Getty Images, and it offers full-time, fair-wage employment.
  • Honest drawbacks: It focuses on vision and sensor data rather than frontier LLM post-training by credentialed experts. Pricing is not public.
  • Pricing model: Custom, scoped by project.
  • Official site: sama.com

How to choose an AI training data provider

  1. Name the data type first. LLM post-training (SFT, RLHF preferences, rubrics), agent RL environments, evaluations and red teaming, computer-vision and sensor labels, robotics demonstrations and off-the-shelf datasets are different products. Few vendors are best at all of them.
  2. Match the expertise to the task. Frontier reasoning data needs real domain experts, such as physicians, lawyers and senior engineers. Ask how a vendor vets experts, what it pays them, and who reviews their work.
  3. Run a paid pilot against your own evals. Ask for samples (Turing, Snorkel and others offer them), score them against a gold set, and measure inter-annotator agreement and model lift. Surge's pay-if-it-improves offer for off-the-shelf data is one way to cap that risk.
  4. Check neutrality and confidentiality. If you compete with a vendor's investor or largest customer, ask about data segregation, project-staffing walls and contractual protections.
  5. Verify security and compliance. For regulated data, look for documented SOC 2, ISO 27001 and HIPAA coverage (iMerit lists these) and review each vendor's trust center.
  6. Fit the buying model to your scale. Startups and academics can begin with self-serve platforms such as Prolific or Toloka. Large programs usually work under statements of work with managed teams.
  7. Consider workforce ethics. Ask about pay rates, rate stability and working conditions. Some vendors publish their rates (Mercor lists an average; Handshake posts its rates upfront), and Sama is a Certified B Corporation.

Frequently asked questions

What is an AI training data provider?

An AI training data provider is a company that supplies the human-created or human-verified data used to train and evaluate AI models. That includes labeled images and sensor data, expert-written prompts and answers, RLHF preference rankings, rubrics, red-teaming results, agent trajectories and RL environments.

Who is the biggest AI training data provider in 2026?

By latest reported revenue, Mercor, Surge AI, Handshake AI, micro1 and Scale AI form the top tier. Scale AI has the highest reported valuation after Meta's 2025 investment. Most of these companies are private, so the figures are unaudited and come from different reporting periods.

Is Scale AI still independent after Meta's investment?

Meta bought a 49% stake for a reported $14.3B in June 2025, and founder Alexandr Wang joined Meta. Scale says it remains an independent company. Reports say OpenAI and Google wound down their work with Scale afterwards, so buyers should do their own confidentiality diligence.

How much does AI training data cost?

Most providers quote custom prices. Prolific is the main exception: you pay the participant reward you set (at least $8/hr, with $12/hr recommended) plus a platform fee of about 42.8% for corporate or 33.3% for academic customers. Expert work costs more; Mercor lists a $90/hr average contracted rate for experts.

What is RLHF data and who provides it?

RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) data is human rankings or ratings of model outputs, used to train reward models and align model behavior. Surge AI, Scale AI, Mercor, Handshake AI, Labelbox, Toloka, iMerit and Appen all offer it, and Prolific supports preference collection.

What is the difference between a labeling platform and a managed data provider?

A labeling platform is software your own team or workforce uses to create labels. A managed provider supplies the expert workforce, project management and quality assurance and delivers finished datasets. Labelbox, iMerit, Toloka and Prolific offer both.

Which providers are best for robotics and physical AI data?

Scale AI (physical AI), Labelbox Terra (egocentric and teleoperated demonstrations), micro1 (robotics data), Mercor (robotics), Toloka (physical AI and egocentric video), iMerit (robotics and LiDAR) and Sama (robotics and manufacturing vision) all offer robotics data.

Can I buy off-the-shelf AI training datasets?

Yes. Surge AI, Mercor, Toloka and Appen sell off-the-shelf datasets, and Snorkel sells its Snorkel Data Series. Off-the-shelf data is faster and cheaper than custom work but less tailored to your model's specific failure modes.

Which provider is best for a small budget or academic research?

Prolific is the most accessible: no contract or subscription, pay as you go, and a discounted 33.3% fee for academic and non-profit customers. Toloka also offers a self-service agreement.

How do I evaluate a training data vendor's quality?

Request samples, run a small paid pilot, score the output against your own gold set and evals, measure agreement between annotators, and check whether training on the data actually improves your target metrics. Also confirm how the vendor vets experts and handles security.
